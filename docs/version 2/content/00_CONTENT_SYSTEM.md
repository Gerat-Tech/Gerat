# Gerat Website Content System - Version 2

## Purpose

This document is the master content rulebook for the Gerat website redesign. AI agents must use it before editing any page copy.

Gerat is a young software and IT solutions company founded in Addis Ababa in 2026. The website should present the company as credible, capable, and intentional without pretending that Gerat already has a large portfolio, years of history, awards, or enterprise-scale proof that it does not yet have.

## Core brand idea

**Gerat connects businesses to useful technology.**

The brand presentation positions Gerat around four ideas:
- **Support** - technology should support people and business growth.
- **Bridge** - Gerat connects businesses with the digital world.
- **Scalability** - solutions should be able to grow with the business.
- **Founders** - five founders brought together by one vision.

The visual identity brief describes Gerat as a new venture providing custom website development, branding, and system engineering for businesses and partners looking for reliable, high-quality digital tools and infrastructure. Its stated keywords are scalable, professional, functional, tech-forward, and reliable.

## Name story

The brand can use the meaning of the German word **Gerät** as a light brand reference. In German, *Gerät* means a tool, device, apparatus, or piece of equipment. Gerat should not claim that "Gerat" is the exact German spelling; the standard spelling is "Gerät". The brand story can frame the name as an inspiration for the idea that software should be useful like a well-made tool: purposeful, practical, and built to do a job.

Recommended wording:

> **Gerat takes its name inspiration from the German word _Gerät_ - a tool or device made for a purpose. That idea is simple: technology should be useful.**

Use this idea mainly on the About page. Do not repeat it across the site.

## Voice

Use:
- direct language
- strong verbs
- short sentences
- confident but grounded statements
- human language
- clear business outcomes
- technical terms only when they improve clarity

Avoid:
- corporate filler
- exaggerated claims
- fake authority
- long paragraphs
- repetitive service descriptions
- empty phrases such as "cutting-edge", "world-class", "game-changing", "revolutionary", "seamless", "future-proof", "mission-critical", or "next-generation" unless a specific claim is actually supported

## Content length rules

The website should feel easy to scan.

- Hero headline: 4-10 words.
- Hero supporting copy: 15-30 words.
- Normal section introduction: 25-60 words.
- Service card: 15-35 words.
- Process step: 10-30 words.
- Principle/value: 2-8 words plus one short explanation.
- CTA: 2-6 words.
- Avoid paragraphs longer than 3 lines on desktop where possible.

One section = one idea.

## Truthfulness rules for AI agents

Do NOT invent:
- clients
- testimonials
- awards
- revenue
- user counts
- uptime figures
- completed projects
- case-study results
- certifications
- partnerships
- years of experience
- team size beyond the known five founders

Do not present concept projects or internal/future products as completed client work.

Because Gerat is starting in 2026, use the founding team, clear capability, point of view, process, and direction as the main trust signals.

2. About

4. Contact

### Remove from the current navigation

- Portfolio - replace with About for now. Add a Work/Case Studies section later when Gerat has genuine projects worth showing.
- Insights - remove for now. Reintroduce only when Gerat has a real publishing rhythm and original material to share.
- Standalone Team page - merge the founders/team into About unless the site later grows enough to justify a separate page.

## Homepage narrative principle

The homepage should feel like one story, not a collection of unrelated blocks.

Recommended journey:

**What we believe -> why we exist -> what we build -> how we work -> who is behind it -> what happens next**

The scroll should progressively answer the visitor's questions without repeating earlier sections.

## Research patterns used

The content architecture is based on recurring patterns observed in current software/company sites:

- Clear positioning before detail.
- A short origin story that explains why the company exists.
- A small number of strong principles instead of a long values list.
- Services grouped into understandable categories.
- A visible process that makes the engagement easier to understand.
- Team/founder context as a trust signal.
- Future direction presented as intent, not as proof of results.
- Strong, direct CTA language.

Current examples reviewed include Replit, Linear, Notion, Ramp, UTUBAA Systems, and current Framer software/agency templates. These patterns support clarity, story, process, values, and team context without requiring every section to be on the homepage.
